Volume I: The Sovereign Pour-Over Last Will and Testament

The Pour-Over Will serves as the essential safety net in sovereign estate planning. It guarantees that any property inadvertently omitted from your Trust during life is automatically poured into the Trust upon transition:

================================================================================ LAST WILL AND TESTAMENT (POUR-OVER WILL) OF [TESTATOR FULL LEGAL NAME] ================================================================================ I, [TESTATOR FULL LEGAL NAME], residing at [ADDRESS], declare that this is my Last Will and Testament, hereby revoking all prior wills and codicils made by me. ARTICLE I: FAMILY DECLARATIONS I declare that I am married to [SPOUSE NAME], and all references to my spouse are to them. I have [NUMBER] living children: [CHILDREN NAMES]. ARTICLE II: POUR-OVER GIFT TO TRUST I give, devise, and bequeath all the rest, residue, and remainder of my estate, both real and personal, of whatever kind and wherever situated, to the then-acting Trustee of that certain Trust known as "[NAME OF TRUST]", created under an Indenture dated [DATE OF TRUST], prior to the execution of this Will. Said residuary estate shall be added to the principal of the Trust and administered, invested, and distributed in accordance with all the terms, conditions, and provisions of said Trust Indenture, including any amendments thereto made prior to my death. ARTICLE III: APPOINTMENT OF EXECUTOR I appoint [EXECUTOR FULL LEGAL NAME] as the Executor of this my Last Will and Testament. If they fail or cease to act, I appoint [ALTERNATE EXECUTOR NAME] as Successor Executor. I direct that no Executor nominated herein shall be required to post bond or other security in any jurisdiction for the faithful performance of their duties. IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have subscribed my name this [DAY] day of [MONTH], 20[YEAR]. _________________________________________________ [TESTATOR SIGNATURE], Testator

⚠️ ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED — EDUCATIONAL SKELETON — NOT FOR EXECUTION ⚠️

2026 exemption context (verified Nov. 18, 2026): $15,000,000 per person; $19,000 annual exclusion. Married couple can move up to $30M gift-tax-free with SLATs. Each spouse retains *indirect* access through the other's trust. Ethics note: separate counsel for each spouse is mandatory practice — these are two instruments, not one.

TRUST A — HUSBAND AS GRANTOR (SKELETON)

TRUST AGREEMENT — [NAME] SLAT TRUST A U/A/D [DATE]

GRANTOR: [Husband]. TRUSTEE: [Corporate Trustee — NOT Wife, NOT Husband]. DISTRIBUTION TRUSTEE (for Wife's benefit): [independent person]. TRUST PROTECTOR: [NAME]. BENEFICIARIES: (1) Wife (discretionary beneficiary for life), (2) Grantor's issue, per stirpes.

KEY CLAUSES:

1. IRREVOCABILITY & NO BENEFICIAL INTEREST OF GRANTOR — Irrevocable; Grantor is *not* a beneficiary; Grantor reserves no § 2036/§ 2038 interest.

2. WIFE'S INTEREST — ANTI-INCLUSION DISCIPLINE — Wife is a discretionary beneficiary only; if Wife ever serves as trustee, her power is limited to an HEMS ascertainable standard exercisable only with a non-adverse party (non-general power — § 2041 safe). No general power of appointment in any beneficiary. Wife's interest terminates on divorce (divorce-protection clause: alternate beneficiaries = Grantor's issue; consider also: Wife's interest as non-assignable).

3. CRUMMEY WITHDRAWAL — 5-and-5 withdrawal rights (IRC § 2514(c)); notice + log discipline (Vol. XVI § 3).

4. GRANTOR-TRUST TOGGLE — [Grantor trust powered (§§ 675(4)(C) swap, 677 variants excluding spouse-income trap) / non-grantor]. Coordinate with tax counsel.

5. DISTRIBUTIONS — For Wife: HEMS only, by DT (not exercisable by Wife); for Issue: broader discretionary standard [health, education, support, *and* comfort/best interests]; spray powers; no distribution to Excluded Persons (creditors, spouses of issue [optional], Claimants).

6. SPENDTHRIFT & ANTI-ALIENATION — Full spendthrift; interests non-assignable; anti-duress clause.

7. TRUSTEE SUCCESSION & PROTECTOR — Ladder [1st Corporate; 2nd ___; 3rd ___]; Protector may remove/replace, amend for tax law (decanting-compatible), change situs.

8. SITUS & GOVERNING LAW — [SD / DE / NV / WY — verify statute]; exclusive jurisdiction in situs courts; migration clause.

9. TAX CLAUSES — GST formula allocation on timely 709; estate-tax apportionment; in terrorem [verify situs enforceability].

10. TERMINATION — On Wife's death (if issue all grown): continue for issue in further trusts / distribute per age tiers [DESIGN DECISION — PLACEHOLDER].

TRUST B — WIFE AS GRANTOR (ANTI-RECIPROCITY DISCIPLINE)

Mirror Trust A with deliberate differences — the Reciprocal Trust Doctrine (*United States v. Grace*, 395 U.S. 316 (1969)) ignores cross-gifts that are "mutual in every detail." Vary:

1. Different trustee (different corporate fiduciary).

2. Different distribution standard for Husband (e.g., HEMS + [education-of-grandchildren] vs. HEMS-only).

3. Different remainder/timing (Trust B: no age-tier; Trust A: age-tier at [35/40/45] — pick one design).

4. Different initial funding ($[X] vs. $[Y] — not equal).

5. Staggered funding dates ([date 1] vs. [date 2 + 30 days]).

6. Different protector (different institution/individual).

EXECUTION CHECKLIST (per trust): separate counsel engagement letters; Form 709 (year 1: both gifts; allocate GST via formula); Crummey notices; funding schedule; solvency review; coordination with ILIT/dynasty layers.

SKELETON — IRREVOCABLE LIFE INSURANCE TRUST (ILIT)

⚠️ ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED — EDUCATIONAL SKELETON — NOT FOR EXECUTION ⚠️

Design core: the policy is owned by the trust from inception (or gifted in within the § 2035 3-year window discipline), so the death benefit escapes § 2042 estate inclusion while providing estate liquidity. Insured must hold NO incidents of ownership — never trustee, never a withdrawal-right holder on their own policy.

---

TRUST AGREEMENT — [NAME] INSURANCE TRUST U/A/D [DATE]

GRANTOR(S): [Insured] (per-policy trusts recommended for multi-insured couples — joint-life/second-to-die policy may use one trust; verify second-to-die funding mechanics per spouse's estate plan).

TRUSTEE: [Corporate/independent trustee — NOT the insured, NOT the insured's spouse, NOT anyone obligated to pay estate taxes (§ 2042(1)(B) incidents-of-ownership trap)].

BENEFICIARIES: Grantor's issue, per stirpes (+ Spouse as discretionary beneficiary [DESIGN DECISION — if Spouse is a beneficiary, income may not be applied to premiums on the Spouse's insurable interest without care — § 677(a)(3) grants income applied to *insured's* premiums creates grantor status, which is the desired trigger; spouse-as-beneficiary is generally fine]).

KEY CLAUSES:

1. IRREVOCABLE / NO RETAINED INTEREST — Irrevocable; Grantor reserves no right to alter; Protector holds replacement power.

2. GRANTOR-TRUST POWERS (intentional) — (a) Trustee (a non-adverse party) may apply income/principal to pay premiums on the Grantor's life (§ 677(a)(3)); (b) Substitution power § 675(4)(C) (swap equivalent-value assets; trustee, not grantor, holds it); (c) borrow without adequate interest/security § 675(2) [optional]. Result: premiums effectively paid with the Grantor's dollars without gift tax on the income-tax outlay.

3. POLICY OWNERSHIP & FUNDING

▪Trust is applicant, owner, and beneficiary of policy(ies) on Grantor's life: [insurer; policy nos. — PLACEHOLDER].
▪Premium funding: annual gifts ($19,000 exclusion-tier × beneficiaries, 2026 — verified; Crummey-funded) → Trustee pays insurer directly.
▪Existing policy transfer: must occur 3+ years before death (§ 2035 pullback) or expect inclusion; gift of an existing policy to the insured's ILIT is excepted from transfer-for-value as a gift carryover-basis transfer (IRC § 101(a)(2)(C)) — confirm with tax counsel; 7122? no — file Form 712 at transfer; no § 698? — skip.

4. CRUMMEY WITHDRAWAL — 5-and-5 (§ 2514(c)); notices to all beneficiaries (guardians for minors); log; unexercised rights lapse; no beneficiary holds withdrawal right on *their own* insured life [if individual policies].

5. TRUSTEE POWERS — Pay premiums; borrow against policy (premium financing note terms: [PLACEHOLDER]); hold other assets; estate-liquidity mechanics: Trustee may (a) lend to Grantor's estate, (b) purchase assets from the estate, (c) redeem stock to fund redemption obligations [buy-sell coordination — PLACEHOLDER].

7. SPENDTHRIFT / ANTI-ALIENATION / NO CONTEST — Standard suite [verify situs enforceability].

8. SITUS / GOVERNING LAW / MIGRATION — [DE / SD / NV — verify]; Trustee may change situs with Protector consent.

9. ADMINISTRATION — Trustee obtains EIN (grantor trust — income on Grantor's 1040; no 1041); annual: premium calendar, Crummey notices, 709 exclusions, policy service review, [illustration monitoring — fiduciary prudence (UPIA)].

Part V: Third-Party Special Needs & Supplemental Care Trust (SNT) Indenture

SKELETON — THIRD-PARTY SPECIAL NEEDS TRUST (SNT)

⚠️ ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED — EDUCATIONAL SKELETON — NOT FOR EXECUTION ⚠️

Design core: funded by parents/third parties (NOT the beneficiary) → no Medicaid payback requirement (contrast first-party (d)(4)(A) trusts, which require payback, or (d)(4)(C) pooled trusts — see § 7). The entire drafting objective: supplement means-tested benefits (SSI/Medicaid) without disqualifying the beneficiary.

---

TRUST AGREEMENT — [NAME] SPECIAL NEEDS TRUST U/A/D [DATE]

GRANTORS: [Parents/family members] TRUSTEE: [professional/corporate trustee — NOT the beneficiary, NOT a sole beneficiary-controlled party]. STANDBY/SUCCESSOR TRUSTEE: [ladder]. TRUST PROTECTOR/CARE ADVOCATE: [NAME]. BENEFICIARY: [NAME], a person with a disability (definition below).

KEY CLAUSES:

1. DEFINITIONS — "Government Benefits": SSI, Medicaid, Medicaid waiver programs, housing subsidies, and any other needs-based programs. "Disability": [SSA definition / instrument-defined]. "Supplement" — the governing verb of this trust.

2. SUPPLEMENT-NOT-SUPPLANT PURPOSE CLAUSE (the heart) — Trustee shall distribute income and principal only to supplement, and never to supplant or reduce, Government Benefits; distributions shall be made so as to preserve eligibility; no distribution of cash to the Beneficiary; no payment for items of support otherwise provided by Government Benefits (in-kind shelter: rent/food/utilities paid directly can reduce SSI — the "in-kind support and maintenance" rules; the Care Advocate coordinates with the benefits plan).

3. DISTRIBUTION AUTHORITY (what this trust is FOR) — Trustee may pay third parties directly for: supplemental medical/dental/therapies not covered by Medicaid; equipment; home purchase or modifications [held as exempt-asset strategies — coordinate]; transportation/vehicle; travel, recreation, cultural activities; companion/advocate services; insurance premiums; education; legal fees; and quality-of-life enrichment — all subject to Clause 2.

4. PROHIBITED DISTRIBUTIONS — No distributions to or for the direct benefit of [other persons who might be duty-support relatives — anti-support-substitution]; no assets to the Beneficiary's estate; no governmental-agency payback on termination (third-party trust — remains available only because no government claim attaches; contrast Vol. note § 7).

5. SPENDTHRIFT & ANTI-ALIENATION — Beneficiary may not assign, anticipate, pledge, or otherwise alienate; creditors of the Beneficiary (other than statutory claimants) may not reach trust property; anti-SNAP? — anti-"transfer-of-asset" coordination clause: Trustee shall not make transfers that create penalty-period disqualification without written benefits-counsel sign-off.

6. TRUSTEE POWERS & ADMINISTRATION — Full investment powers (UPIA); separate books; annual accounting to Protector/family (§ 813(b) discipline); benefits-eligibility reviews [annual, with counsel]; letter-of-intent maintenance (care preferences — Exhibit B); 1041 filings; no 3520 (domestic trust).

7. FIRST-PARTY COMPARISON NOTE (drafting integrity check) — If ANY asset of the disabled person funds this trust: STOP — that is a first-party trust requiring (a) payback-to-state clause per 42 U.S.C. § 1396p(d)(4)(A), (b) under-65 funding, or a (d)(4)(C) pooled-trust account, drafted to statute — do not use this third-party skeleton.

8. TRUSTEE SUCCESSION / CARE CONTINUITY — Ladder of successor trustees; Care Advocate succession; standby guardianship coordination; trust remains until Beneficiary's death, then remainder to [other children/issue — DESIGN DECISION].

9. GOVERNING LAW / SITUS — [Family's state — verify state SNT statutes and 1396p conforming amendments]; probate-court reporting where state-required.

10. SEVERABILITY / MISCELLANEOUS — Standard suite; in terrorem [verify enforceability].

EXHIBITS: A — Benefits inventory (current SSI/Medicaid status); B — Letter of Intent (care, preferences, providers); C — Distribution decision tree (supplemental-only screening); D — Trustee's benefits-preservation checklist.

---

INSTRUMENT 1 — CHARITABLE REMAINDER TRUST (CRAT or CRUT)

GRANTOR(S): [NAME(S)]. TRUSTEE: [individual/corporate]. CHARITABLE REMAINDERMAN(S): [§ 170(c) organizations — 501(c)(3) public charities only; no private foundations unless 35%-test satisfied].

1. PAYOUT DESIGN

▪CRAT: fixed dollar annuity [__]% of initial funding (5% minimum; verify 7520-based deduction); probability-of-exhaustion test compliance.
▪CRUT: fixed [__]% of annually-revalued trust value [net-income with make-up (NIMCRUT) variant for cash-flow-poor assets].
▪Test actuarial 10% remainder at funding (exhibit: actuary letter).

2. FUNDING & THE CAPITAL-GAINS WIN — Appreciated securities/real estate contributed in kind; CRT sells tax-free (§ 664(c) exemption) and reinvests — grantor bypasses embedded gain; income taxed to grantor/beneficiaries under the 4-tier § 664 ordering (ordinary → capital gain → other → principal).

3. CLAUSES — Irrevocable; no grantor-trust status (must be non-grantor to qualify); trustee powers limited (prudent investor; no self-dealing beyond § 664(d)(2)(B) narrow sale rules); annual Form 5227; Form 1041-R? — CRT files 1041 with § 664 characterizations; deduction substantiation (Form 8283; qualified appraisal for >$5,000 — verify current threshold).

4. TERMINATION — On expiration: remainder to charity(ies) per formula [fixed shares].

COMMON-LAW EXPRESS TRUSTS (PURE TRUSTS / MASSACHUSETTS TRUSTS) — CONTROLLING SUPREME COURT & HISTORICAL JURISPRUDENCE

1. Foundational Definition and Constitutional Anchor:

An Express Trust under the Common Law (historically termed a Common-Law Pure Trust or Massachusetts Trust) is a private contractual fiduciary relationship created by a Declaration or Indenture of Trust whereby a Settlor/Grantor conveys legal title in property (the Trust Res or Corpus) to one or more Trustees to hold, manage, and administer for the equitable benefit of designated Beneficiaries (or Certificate Holders). Its contractual obligation is protected under Article I, Section 10, Clause 1 of the United States Constitution ('No State shall... pass any... Law impairing the Obligation of Contracts') and traditional Chancery Equity jurisprudence.

2. Alfred D. Chandler — Express Trusts under the Common Law (1912):

Alfred Dupont Chandler's landmark 1912 treatise, 'Express Trusts under the Common Law: A Superior and Distinct Mode of Administration, Distinguished from Partnerships, Contrasted with Corporations', established the classic doctrinal distinction between statutory corporations (which derive their existence from a state legislative franchise) and common-law express trusts (which derive their existence from the natural and common-law right of sui juris individuals to contract and convey property to fiduciaries in equity). Under Chandler's doctrine, when Trustees hold full legal title and plenary management control without partner-style control by beneficiaries, the arrangement is a true trust in equity rather than a partnership or corporate franchise.
