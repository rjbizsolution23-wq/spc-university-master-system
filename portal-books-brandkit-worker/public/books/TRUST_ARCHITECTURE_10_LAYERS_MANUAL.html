3. Real Estate Conveyance into Trust — Garn-St. Germain Federal Due-on-Sale Exemption (12 U.S.C. § 1701j-3(d)(8)):

When transferring mortgaged residential real property (1 to 4 dwelling units) into a trust, federal law under the Garn-St. Germain Depository Institutions Act of 1982, 12 U.S.C. § 1701j-3(d)(8), expressly PROHIBITS a mortgage lender from exercising a 'due-on-sale' acceleration clause on a transfer into an inter vivos trust in which the borrower is and remains a beneficiary and which does not relate to a transfer of rights of occupancy in the property. For non-residential or pure asset-holding trusts where the settlor is severed, properties are either transferred free-and-clear, transferred into an Illinois/Florida-style Land Trust or Single-Purpose Holding LLC with lender notice/consent, or encumbered via equity-stripping friendly UCC-1 / Deed of Trust liens in favor of the Master Holding Trust.

4. Illinois-Style & Florida-Style Real Estate Land Trusts (Fla. Stat. § 689.071 / 765 ILCS 405/):

In a Land Trust, a Deed in Trust conveys both legal and equitable title to real estate to the Land Trustee, while the beneficiary holds a personal-property beneficial interest in the proceeds and avails of the real estate. This converts a real estate interest into personal property (which can be assigned privately to the Master Pure Holding Trust via an unrecorded Assignment of Beneficial Interest without public county deed recording of the ultimate Holding Trust) and shields the owner's identity from public county tax-assessor searches.

WORLD, INTERNATIONAL & OFFSHORE ASSET PROTECTION TRUST STATUTORY COMPENDIUM

1. Cook Islands — International Trusts Act 1984 (as amended):

▪Section 13B (Fraudulent Dispositions): A creditor challenging a transfer to a Cook Islands International Trust must prove BEYOND A REASONABLE DOUBT (the criminal standard of proof, rather than civil preponderance of the evidence) in the High Court of the Cook Islands that the transfer was made with the principal intent to defraud that specific creditor and rendered the settlor insolvent.
▪Statute of Limitations (Section 13B(3)): Claims are completely time-barred if the transfer occurred before the creditor's cause of action accrued, or within 1 year from the date of the settlement/transfer (or 2 years from the date the creditor's cause of action accrued).
▪Non-Recognition of Foreign Judgments (Section 13D): The High Court of the Cook Islands is statutorily prohibited from recognizing or enforcing any foreign court judgment against a Cook Islands International Trust, its trustees, or its corpus; a creditor must re-litigate the entire claim ab initio in Rarotonga using local Cook Islands counsel (where contingency-fee arrangements are generally prohibited).
▪Abolition of Rule Against Perpetuities & Forced Heirship (Sections 6 & 13E-13F): Cook Islands trusts may be perpetual and expressly override foreign forced-heirship or matrimonial claims.

2. Nevis — Nevis International Exempt Trust Ordinance (NIETO) 1994 (as amended):

▪Creditor Bond Requirement (Section 55): Before any creditor can file a legal action against a Nevis International Exempt Trust in the High Court of St. Kitts and Nevis, the creditor must deposit a cash bond of $100,000 USD ($270,000 ECD) with the Nevis Ministry of Finance to secure the trustee's legal costs.
▪Burden of Proof & Statute of Limitations (Section 24): Beyond a reasonable doubt standard; 1-year to 2-year strict statute of limitations; total non-recognition of foreign judgments; no Rule Against Perpetuities; seamless pairing with a Nevis LLC (Nevis Limited Liability Company Ordinance 2017, where a charging order is the sole and exclusive remedy and expires after 3 years).

3. Belize — Belize Trusts Act (Chapter 202, Revised Edition):

▪Immediate Asset Protection (Section 7(6)): Unlike jurisdictions with a 1-to-4-year lookback window, Section 7(6) of the Belize Trusts Act provides that the High Court of Belize shall not vary or set aside a Belize trust or disposition into trust on account of creditor claims, bankruptcy, or foreign court orders once the trust is validly settled (absent actual proven fraud at inception), and foreign judgments are statutorily unenforceable.

4. Cayman Islands — Trusts Act, STAR Trusts (Part VIII) & Private Trust Companies (PTC):

▪STAR Trusts (Special Trusts Alternative Regime, Part VIII of the Cayman Islands Trusts Act): Permits trusts for objects or purposes (charitable or non-charitable), or for persons, or both. Critically, under a STAR Trust, standing to enforce the trust is vested exclusively in an appointed 'Enforcer' rather than the beneficiaries—meaning beneficiaries have no direct enforceable property right that a hostile court could compel them to exercise!
▪Cayman Private Trust Company (PTC): A family may incorporate its own Cayman Private Trust Company to serve as trustee of a group of connected family trusts, allowing family governance participation on the PTC board alongside professional fiduciary directors.

5. European Civil-Law Foundations & Hague Trust Convention:

▪Liechtenstein Foundation (Stiftung under PGR Art. 552) & Liechtenstein Trust (Treuhand under PGR Art. 897): The premier Continental European civil-law common-law-equivalent trust and private-purpose foundation vehicles.
▪Hague Convention on the Law Applicable to Trusts and on their Recognition (1985): Ratified by the UK, Switzerland, Italy, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Canada, Australia, Hong Kong, Monaco, Cyprus, and Panama, guaranteeing that common-law express trusts are recognized as separate fiduciary estates even inside civil-law banking jurisdictions like Switzerland.

1. Foundational Definition and Constitutional Anchor:

An Express Trust under the Common Law (historically termed a Common-Law Pure Trust or Massachusetts Trust) is a private contractual fiduciary relationship created by a Declaration or Indenture of Trust whereby a Settlor/Grantor conveys legal title in property (the Trust Res or Corpus) to one or more Trustees to hold, manage, and administer for the equitable benefit of designated Beneficiaries (or Certificate Holders). Its contractual obligation is protected under Article I, Section 10, Clause 1 of the United States Constitution ('No State shall... pass any... Law impairing the Obligation of Contracts') and traditional Chancery Equity jurisprudence.

2. Alfred D. Chandler — Express Trusts under the Common Law (1912):

Alfred Dupont Chandler's landmark 1912 treatise, 'Express Trusts under the Common Law: A Superior and Distinct Mode of Administration, Distinguished from Partnerships, Contrasted with Corporations', established the classic doctrinal distinction between statutory corporations (which derive their existence from a state legislative franchise) and common-law express trusts (which derive their existence from the natural and common-law right of sui juris individuals to contract and convey property to fiduciaries in equity). Under Chandler's doctrine, when Trustees hold full legal title and plenary management control without partner-style control by beneficiaries, the arrangement is a true trust in equity rather than a partnership or corporate franchise.

1. The canon-law tradition (historical foundation)

▪Corpus Juris Canonici (Gratian's Decretum and successors) — the Church's private-law system for clergy discipline, marriage, and benefices (12th–16th c.) [K].
▪England: ecclesiastical courts (consistory, archdeaconry) held jurisdiction over marriage, wills, and probate until the mid-19th century (parish "courts Christian"); the 1857 Matrimonial Causes Act transferred marriage/divorce to civil courts [K].
▪America: no national establishment (First Amendment, 1791); state establishments ended by 1833 (Massachusetts last). Ecclesiastical courts as organs of state power do not exist in the United States — what exists is *church autonomy*, a set of constitutional doctrines protecting voluntary religious associations [K].

2. The American church-autonomy doctrine (the real "ecclesiastical law")

▪Legitimate: church/charitable trusts are real (UTC charitable-trust provisions; Restatement (Third) of Trusts ch. 26); *Jones v. Wolf*'s neutral-principles route expressly contemplates church trusts recorded in corporate charters and deeds [K].
▪Flagged [V]: "ecclesiastical trust," "pure trust," "sovereign trust," "constitutional trust" packages marketed for tax evasion are the IRS-documented Abusive Trust Tax Evasion Schemes; DOJ enjoined promoters (2002). ▪Religious arbitration: beth din and church tribunals operate as *contractual* arbitration under state arbitration acts — their awards are enforceable only through the same civil machinery as any other arbitration, within constitutional limits (paraphrase of settled arbitration law [K]).

▪*Employment Division v. Smith*, 494 U.S. 872 (1990): neutral, generally applicable laws prevail over free-exercise challenges [K].
▪*United States v. Lee*, 455 U.S. 252 (1982) [V]: Amish employer's religious objection did not exempt him from Social Security taxes.

A. MAXIMS OF EQUITY (with their real, citable operation)

1. "Equity will not suffer a wrong to be without a remedy." Origin of equitable relief where law's remedies were inadequate; now embodied in remedies discretion (e.g., specific performance, injunctions — Fed. R. Civ. P. 65; 28 U.S.C. § 2201 (declaratory relief)).

2. "Equity follows the law." Equity supplements, never abolishes, legal rights — the reason the 1938 merger of law and equity (Fed. R. Civ. P. 2) preserved *substantive* distinctions.

3. "He who seeks equity must do equity." Relief conditioned on the plaintiff's own conduct — regularly invoked against litigants advancing pseudo-instruments.

4. "He who comes into equity must come with clean hands." Conduct-based bar; the doctrine courts deploy against parties whose filings were themselves fraudulent.

5. "Equity delights to do justice, not by halves." Full relief framework (Rule 65(d) scope; supplemental decrees).

6. "Equity looks to intent rather than form." True — and the reason *formal labels* ("I declared myself a trust") do not control substance.

7. "Equity regards as done that which ought to be done." Constructive trusts and equitable liens rest here (Restatement (Third) of Restitution).

8. "Equity imputes an intention to fulfill an obligation." Performance of duties presumed in fiduciary relationships.

9. "Equity acts in personam." Personal orders against parties — and the basis of contempt powers against litigants regardless of claimed status.

10. "Equity aids the vigilant, not those who slumber." Laches (Petrella v. Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, 572 U.S. 663 (2014) — its scope against statutes of limitation).

11. "Where equities are equal, the law prevails." Legal title and priority rules decide stale contests.

12. "Between equal equities, priority in time prevails." BFP v. Resolution Trust Corp., 511 U.S. 531 (1994) (context: foreclosures).

13. "Equality is equity." Marshaling and pro-rata doctrines in creditor law.

14. "Once a trustee, always a trustee." Trustee accountability follows the office.

B. TRUST LAW — THE REAL ARCHITECTURE

Elements (Restatement (Third) of Trusts; Uniform Trust Code § 402): (1) *settlor* with capacity and intent; (2) ascertainable *beneficiary* (with charitable-trust exceptions); (3) identified *trust property* (corpus); (4) *trustee* with duties; (5) valid *purpose*.

Types: express, resulting, constructive (imposed by law against fraud/breach — e.g., fraudulent conveyances, 11 U.S.C. § 548), testamentary, inter vivos, charitable, and business/escrow variants.

Fiduciary duties: loyalty (no self-dealing), prudence (UTC § 804), impartiality (§ 803), duty to account (§ 813). Breach remedies: surcharge, disgorgement, removal (§ 706), constructive trust.

Persona-relevant caution (verified): trusts marketed as "pure," "constitutional," "sovereign," "ecclesiastical," or "unincorporated business organization" packages for hiding income/assets are the IRS-documented Abusive Trust Tax Evasion Schemes (IRS pages current 2026; DOJ injunctions 2002). Red flags per IRS: grantor retains control; no independent trustee; "trust" files nothing. Legitimate church autonomy (*Watson v. Jones*, 80 U.S. 679 (1871); *Hosanna-Tabor*, 565 U.S. 171 (2012); *Our Lady of Guadalupe*, 591 U.S. 732 (2020)) concerns internal church governance — it does not shield secular assets or income.

C. COMMERCIAL LAW — UCC ARTICLES 1, 3, 9 (OPERATIVE REAL CONTENT)

Article 1: "Good faith" = honesty in fact (1-201(b)(20)); "person" = individual or organization (1-201(b)(30)); freedom-of-contract (1-302).

Article 3 — Negotiable Instruments: elements (3-104: unconditional promise/order to pay a fixed sum, to order or bearer, on demand or definite time, signed); negotiation (3-201); holder in due course (3-302: value, good faith, no notice of claims/defects); liability requires signature (3-401); presentment, notice of dishonor (3-503), protest (3-501). The A4V/"signature is an endorsement" inversion has no support in any of these sections — liability attaches by *signature on an instrument*, not by an instrument's existence.

Article 9 — Secured Transactions: attachment (9-203: value given; debtor rights in collateral; authenticated agreement); perfection (filing, possession, control — 9-301 et seq.); priority (9-317 et seq.); debtor-name rules (9-503); filing-office integrity — states' responses to fraudulent filings (NASS 2014). "Secured party" is a role in a collateral transaction — not a personal status, and no Article 9 mechanism separates a natural person from liability.

United States*, 318 U.S. 363 (1943) (federal commercial paper law); the Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws and UCC in origin reference the lex mercatoria's absorption. The legitimate "Law Merchant" is this absorbed body of commercial doctrine — not a parallel private jurisdiction.

2. The UCC — Actual Structure

▪A model code drafted by ALI/ULC (first promulgated 1952; Article 9 revised 1998), adopted by all 50 states, D.C., and the territories. It governs private commercial transactions: sales (Art. 2), negotiable instruments (Art. 3), bank deposits (Art. 4), funds transfers (Art. 4A), letters of credit (Art. 5), bulk sales (Art. 6), documents of title (Art. 7), investment securities (Art. 8), secured transactions (Art. 9).
▪Article 9 mechanics: a security interest is created by *agreement* between debtor and secured party (§ 9-203) plus value and attachment. A UCC-1 financing statement (§ 9-502) merely *perfects* an interest that must already exist; filing offices index by debtor name. A filing creates nothing by itself.
▪The pseudo-theory: that a person can file a UCC-1 against the "strawman," the government, or a creditor and thereby perfect a claim to Treasury accounts or discharge debts. Status: REJECTED in every reported instance; filings without an underlying obligation can constitute criminal false-lien conduct (18 U.S.C. §§ 891–896; see Vol. VIII).

3. "Accepted for Value" (A4V) and Instrument Theories

▪A4V claims that endorsing "Accepted for Value, Exemption No. ..." on a bill converts it into a Treasury discharge. No provision of the UCC, the fiscal statutes, or 31 U.S.C. creates any such mechanism. Courts treat A4V defenses as frivolous; taxpayers asserting them face 26 U.S.C. § 6702 penalties ($5,000 per frivolous submission) and § 6673 damages.
▪The scheme's genealogy is verified: the "redemption"/A4V movement traces to Roger Elvick in the 1980s (SPLC profile; a Fourth Circuit opinion documents its Posse Comitatus origins); Elvick was among defendants in *United States v. Lorenzo*, 995 F.2d 1448 (5th Cir. 1993), a federal prosecution arising from fictitious commercial-paper schemes.

4. Trusts — the Real Law (and the Fake Kind)

A valid express trust requires: (1) a settlor with capacity, (2) intent, (3) ascertainable beneficiaries, (4) trust property, (5) a valid purpose (UTC § 402; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 46). Trusts are tax-neutral or tax-transparent entities governed by Subchapter J (26 U.S.C. §§ 641–692) and the grantor-trust rules (§§ 671–679).

Abusive "patriot"/"pure"/"constitutional trust" schemes — marketed as freeing property from taxation or judgment — have been rejected and penalized for decades:

▪*Rodewald v. Commissioner*, 77 T.C. 490 (1981): substance over form — a trustee with no meaningful duties is a "goldfish bowl"; the settlor remains taxable owner.
▪IRS "Abusive Trust Schemes" enforcement program; 26 U.S.C. § 6700 (promoter penalties); criminal convictions for trust-based tax evasion (e.g., *United States v. Anderson*, 444 F.3d 1155 (9th Cir. 2006), "Anderson's Ark" — verify before citing further).
▪Sovereign-trust myths: no trust instrument can remove a person from the jurisdiction of the courts, create a "foreign status," or "capture" government accounts. The grantor-trust rules specifically *ignore* interposed entities when the grantor retains control (§§ 671–677) — the code anticipates and defeats these structures.

5. Government Entities — the Real Definitions

▪Government corporations exist by statute (31 U.S.C. § 9101 list: Amtrak, TVA, FDIC, U.S. Postal Service, etc.). A corporation-for-government-purpose is a creature of Congress — not evidence of a "corporate United States."
▪28 U.S.C. § 3002(15) — the most misquoted definition in America. Text (LII-verified): for purposes of the set-off chapter (18→28 U.S.C. ch. 176 set-off provisions), "'United States' means — (A) a Federal corporation; (B) an agency, department, commission, board, or other entity of the United States; or (C) an instrumentality of the United States." It is a *catch-all definition ensuring the government can be reached in set-off proceedings* — a creditor-friendly, not status-defining, provision. PolitiFact (Feb. 2024) and USA Today (Mar. 2023) fact-checked the "U.S. is a private corporation" claim: false. Courts reject the argument wherever raised.
▪The Act of 1871 created a *municipal corporation for the District of Columbia* — a local government form. A municipal corporation is a common unit of local government; it is not a private company and says nothing about the national government's character. Status of the "corporate U.S." theory: REJECTED/UNSUPPORTED.

6. Public Law vs. Private Law

A jurisprudential distinction (public law = state's relations with persons; private law = persons' relations with each other) — with a modern procedural meaning: "Public Law" is the standard designation of every federal session law (e.g., Pub. L. No. 93-198). The pseudolegal claim that "public law" statutes bind only government or that "private law" secretly governs citizens is UNSUPPORTED.

7. What Legitimate Commercial Law Offers

▪Real secured transactions (UCC-9), real negotiable-instrument defenses (UCC-3), real trusts, real consumer-protection claims (FDCPA 15 U.S.C. § 1692; FCRA), and real bankruptcy relief (11 U.S.C.). These are the lawful paths to debt problems — the redemption toolkit is a path to sanctions and prison.
